Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, “Reliability of scVI and totalVI for CITE-seq under distribution shift and uncertainty-guided prediction,” for publication as a Research article in Genome Biology.

Deep generative models such as scVI and totalVI are widely used to integrate CITE-seq RNA and protein measurements. In practice, reliability under protein-entry degradation, dataset or donor shift, training stochasticity, and uncertainty-based filtering is less systematically characterized than average predictive accuracy. Our study addresses this gap through a structured reliability evaluation of architecture-matched scVI and totalVI (with PCA and MOFA+ baselines).

We evaluate representation quality, entry-wise protein corruption, reciprocal cross-dataset transfer, predictive and latent uncertainty, replicated stochastic variability, and selective prediction with cell-type retention reporting. We then test central findings on an independent Lawlor Baseline CITE-seq cohort using author labels only under donor-held-out validation (100 model runs).

Quantitatively, totalVI improved independent donor-held-out macro-F1 from 0.800 to 0.888 (paired Δ = +0.088; 50/50 fold×seed pairs). Predictive uncertainty remained strongly error-informative, latent posterior variance tracked error in a model-dependent manner, and confidence abstention reduced retained-set risk while creating substantial cell-type retention imbalance. We do not claim a novel fusion architecture; the contribution is systematic reliability characterization with external validation.
